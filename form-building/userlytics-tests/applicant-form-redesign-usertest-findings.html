Applicant Form Redesign: Usability Findings

Seven GLM admins filled in a sample application as if they were applicants, in two layouts: one section at a time, then the full page. Unmoderated remote study (Userlytics). October 2026.

Both layouts improve on today's form. Validation, help text and a few missing controls need work before the build.

What we learned

Mapped against the applicant questions in the onsite scope: wizard or full page, whether people can tell how much is left, and whether they trust that their work is saved. All evidence is from this Userlytics round [UL] unless tagged otherwise. Participants were admins speaking for applicants, so read applicant claims as secondhand.

✓

What we were right about

  • Both layouts are an improvement; no more expanding and collapsing sections 7/7 positive
  • The section list shows how much work there is 6/7
  • Adding budget lines as you go, without deciding rows up front 5/7
  • Errors shown on the field, such as the request cap 4/7
  • Review and submit pointing to what is unanswered 3/7
✗

What we were wrong about

  • Today's controls can wait: collaborator, download and print were missed 4/7
  • An error message is enough; people want the rule stated before they type 3/7
  • Errors behave the same in both layouts. They did not, and it shaped preference 2/7 + build
  • Fields format themselves: dates, currency and uploads all misled someone 3 single reports
!

What surprised us

  • The layout preference leans wizard but is not decisive, and most complaints about the full page were about validation timing
  • Branching that appears right under its question was singled out as fixing a real applicant complaint
  • Sample screening questions read as an eligibility gate, which regular forms are not meant to be [SME]
  • Admins expect a budget upload to stay available alongside the table

What to do next

Carry into the build with confidence

  • Section list on the left with question and required counts per section
  • Sections always open, in both layouts
  • Save and continue, Save and exit, and a visible save status
  • Budget table that grows a line at a time
  • Review and submit with links back to each unanswered question
  • Errors on the field itself, worded as the rule to meet

Iterate before the next round

  • Make errors appear the same way in both layouts, when the applicant leaves a field rather than only on save. (2/7)
  • Add help text to every field with a rule: digits, limits, character counts, file size. (3/7)
  • Restore collaborator, download and print. They exist today. (4/7)
  • Give review and submit more than a bulleted list.
  • Let admins write the confirmation message so it matches their real next steps. (1/7)
  • Add a budget upload option beside the table. (2/7)
  • Fix field defects: year entry on dates, thousands separators on currency, "No file chosen" after an upload, funder and applicant names in the header.
  • Replace the screening-style sample questions with neutral opening questions.

Test in a follow-up round

  • Wizard against full page once errors behave the same, with real applicants from the general panel
  • Whether people can say how much is left, before starting and halfway through
  • Whether people know their work is saved after leaving and coming back
  • Branching under the question against branching into its own section
  • Whether the full-page section list keeps up on a tall screen

Data pulls to inform next steps

  • Branching usage: How many active processes use branching, and how much of it is single-question show and hide? Sizes the branching work. [PX], requested
  • Budget collection: How many application forms collect the budget as an upload, a table, or both? Tells us how much the table changes for applicants. [PX], requested
  • Print and download use: How often do applicants download or print a blank application today? Sets the priority of restoring it. [PX], requested

The evidence behind the top findings

Each finding includes participant quotes and the moment to clip. Participants are referred to by study ID.

Validated (7/7)

Both layouts improve on today's form

Every participant's first impression was positive. The clearest reason was structural: sections are always open, so nobody has to expand and collapse groups to find or answer a question. Participants described the result as less overwhelming and easier to move around.

The four who gave confidence ratings landed between "somewhat smooth" and "smooth and easy" for both layouts. Nobody rated either one as hard.

"I like that it's not like the current where you have to click the little arrow to drop down."

Validated (6/7)

The section list tells people how big the form is

The left-hand list was the most praised element. Participants used it to see the shape of the application before starting, to jump between sections, and to see what was left. The question and required counts under each section were called out by name as a way for applicants to see where to focus.

Two refinements surfaced. The list does not look clickable to everyone, and in the full page the highlight cannot reach the last sections on a tall screen, so it stops tracking where you are.

"I also like on the side that there's like a list of the number of questions and how many are required."

Validated (4/7)

Errors on the field catch problems admins fix by hand today

The $25,000 request cap drew the strongest single reaction. Admins recognized it immediately as a problem they deal with after submission, when applicants ask for more than a program allows. Seeing the error on the field, and the section turn red in the list, was described as a clear improvement.

This backs the in-scope work on minimum, maximum and format rules for a single field (#45).

"I do like that there's like that cap on how much you can request... I do have that issue sometimes where people request more than the maximum of a grant."

Emerging (3/7)

State the rule before people break it

Participants liked the errors but wanted to know the rule before typing. The phone field needed 10 digits and said so only after a wrong entry. File size limits, character counts on the project description, and the maximum request were all expected up front. One participant also wanted the EIN field to stop accepting digits past nine, not just flag them.

The plan already puts a format hint under the input. This round shows it needs to be on every field that has a rule, not just some.

"Knowing that we have to enter 10 digits, it would be nice if it said that... ahead of time before you're frustrated."

Emerging (4/7)

Collaborator, download and print were missed

These controls exist today and were left out of the prototype. Four participants looked for at least one of them. Inviting a collaborator came up most for long applications split across staff. Downloading or printing a blank application came up because applicants like to read every question before starting, which the one-section-at-a-time layout makes harder.

One participant also wanted to write the confirmation message, so it states the foundation's real next steps.

"Sometimes the applicants like to print out or download a blank version before they actually start working, and I don't see that option."

Isolated (2/7), with a build cause

Errors appeared later in the full page, and people noticed

In the wizard, errors appear when a section is saved. In the full page, they only appeared at the end. Two participants entered an over-cap request in the full page, kept going, and found the problem at review. Both preferred the wizard partly for this reason.

This is a choice in the prototype, not a property of the layout. Errors should appear when the applicant leaves a field, in both views, before the layouts are compared again.

"Do I have to wait until the end to find out those things?... If you have a super long grant application, it might be nice to kind of catch those mistakes as you go through."

Isolated (2/7)

Branching under its question fixes a known applicant complaint

In the wizard, the fiscal sponsor branch opened as its own section. In the full page, the follow-up question appeared directly under "Is this a new program?" One participant said applicants regularly miss branched groups that appear several sections later and have to go back to find what triggered them. A second called the branching clean.

This supports single-question show and hide, which is in scope.

"Branching questions to be able to populate right under that question that impacts the answers, also very helpful."

Open question

One section at a time, or the full page?

The feedback leans toward the wizard, but not enough to call it. Three participants preferred the wizard, one preferred the full page, and three had no clear preference. The wizard was shown first on purpose as the baseline, so the full page had to earn its place.

Most complaints about the full page were about errors appearing late, which the build caused. Those do not make the full page worse. Preference for the full page rested on seeing every question and jumping between them, the same reason applicants want a printable copy.

The next round should compare the two once errors behave the same, with applicants rather than admins.

"I think that just having the smaller chunks of the app is helpful."

P6, preferred the wizard

"Having everything in one area is just more easier for me... It's like a table of contents, so you can basically jump to the section that you need more work on."

P1, preferred the full page

Reference

Study design

Unmoderated remote study in Userlytics. Seven GLM admins were asked to act as applicants and fill in a sample seven-section application, then review and submit it. Each saw the one-section-at-a-time layout first and the full-page layout second. The order was fixed on purpose, so the wizard is the baseline. Each layout had first impressions, a "what is missing" question, a fill-in task and a 1 to 5 confidence rating. The builder is being tested separately.

Confidence thresholds (adjusted for n=7)

  • Convergent (5-7 of 7): Act on now.
  • Emerging (3-4 of 7): Document and watch.
  • Isolated (1-2 of 7): Note, don't act yet.

Limitations

  • Participants were admins, not applicants. Everything about applicants is secondhand.
  • Fixed order means preference is mixed with familiarity and fatigue.
  • Four of seven completed both layouts fully. Two gave brief answers, and one lost track of the second task.
  • One session had a second person contributing.
  • Errors appeared at different times in the two layouts, which shaped some preferences.
  • Editing after submission was possible in the prototype but is not allowed in GLM [SME].
  • The sample's screening questions read as an eligibility gate. Eligibility is a separate feature in GLM [SME].
  • No completion times, error counts, or estimates of how much was left were collected.

Participants

Seven GLM admins, P1 to P7. Names are kept out of this page. All study evidence is tagged [UL]; internal context is tagged [SME].

Funder and applicant names were confused in the header (2/7)

P4 and P5 were unsure whether Riverbend Community Fund or Maple Street Arts Collective was their organization. Due date placement added to the confusion.

Budget as an upload (2/7)

P6 asked whether uploading a budget goes away, and P2 noted they collect budgets as an attachment today. P6 noted reviewers like uploaded budgets, and that a standard table might stop applicants attaching their whole organization's budget.

Upload still said "No file chosen" after a file was uploaded (1/7)

P5 was unsure whether the upload worked, and whether a second file could be added.

Year entry on the date field (1/7)

P1 could not type a four-digit year without the cursor jumping, and suggested limiting the year range.

Currency without thousands separators (1/7)

P5 entered $100,000 and wanted the commas shown.

Active section marker in the full page (1/7)

P3 wanted a clearer indicator in the section list for where you are, and an obvious sign that sections are clickable.

Use of space on large monitors (1/7)

P1 saw a lot of empty space and suggested more fields side by side.

Context before starting (1/7)

P4 wanted an introduction with eligibility requirements before the first section, and a countdown to the due date.

Requests outside this epic (1/7 each)

Copying answers from a previous application (P1), notes for collaborators (P1), contacts filled in from the organization (P3), a summed budget total (P5), and a way to abandon an application (P4).

Sample content (1/7)

P7 did not know which "four counties" the question meant. A sample-content issue, fixed with the new opening questions.
