Why this exists

The post ship signal package is still an open team discussion. Most of the measures in it compare against a before state, and the before state stops being available the day the new homepage ships. This page records what that before state looks like now, so the conversation can happen without having lost the chance to answer it.

The number worth carrying into the readout

Nine out of ten people who land on the homepage never touch the LOI grid, and it occupies a quarter of the fixed Workload row. Approval reaches roughly one in five and is trending down. Three of five usability participants asked to remove processes they do not use, and one named LOI directly. That was written up as a convergent finding at 3 of 5. It is stronger than that: it is 3 of 5 corroborated across roughly 3,100 accounts.

Before quoting it, resolve whether LOI is disabled for most organizations or enabled and ignored. Both point to the same fix, hiding unused processes, but they support different arguments. Configuration data answers this and PX does not.

02 · Segmentation

Role is populated, and it is not the role we mean

Role is a first-class user attribute in PX, not a global context field, so every measure on this page can be split by it. The values are the GLM permission role.

What segments cleanly

  • Permission role. Administrator, Grants Manager, Evaluator, Auditor, Board Member, Applicant. Splits every reach and adoption measure.
  • License Type. Populated as a custom user attribute. Lets us watch Standard against Advanced behavior on the new page.
  • Account. Adoption tables give which accounts use which features and at what depth.
  • Tenure. Sign up date, first visit, last seen, and number of visits are all on the user record.
  • Device and browser. Available per user, useful if density or type scale complaints recur after launch.

Where it breaks down

  • There is no Program Officer role in GLM. The persona at the center of our open research question does not exist as a system role. Program officers appear as Administrator or Grants Manager, indistinguishable from anyone else with those permissions.
  • 20% of users have a blank role. Any role split silently drops a fifth of the base unless that bucket is reported.
  • Multi-role users need normalizing. Values arrive as strings like Evaluator,Grants Manager and will not group correctly as-is.
  • Functional Role is the job function field, and coverage looks thin. Maintained by Gainsight CS from Salesforce. Present on only two of thirteen users in the account sampled. Confirm coverage with CS before relying on it.
  • Global context is empty on both page view and feature match events, for GLM and CommunitySuite. Anything we want in the event payload has to be added.
What this does not solve

Role segmentation gives us permission level, not job function. It does not close the role gap in the usability study. The program officer research round still has to happen, because neither PX nor the post ship data can find those people for us.

One number to hand to CS or implementation

84 Board Members across a 20,000 user sample, 0.4%. Board reporting came up directly in the usability study, and board members finding the interface confusing appears in the investment brief. Either board members are not in GLM, they reach it by a path that is not instrumented, or the role is not being assigned. All three matter for the adoption breadth argument and none is answerable from PX.

03 · Instrumentation

What is tagged, and what is not

Of 240 tagged features in GLM/SLM, two mention the homepage. Everything else on this page is inferred from those two plus the workload grids beneath them.

Measurable today, no new work

  • Homepage reach by user and by account, daily or weekly
  • Per grid reach, trend, and share of homepage users
  • Account level adoption tables and per user visit counts
  • Feature ranking within a single account
  • Path analysis in the PX interface, including the homepage to reporting route and the homepage self loop
  • In-app surveys through PX engagements, the same mechanism that recruited the usability study

Not measurable until tags ship

  • Customize open rate and whether editing recurs after week one
  • Which widgets get added, in what order, and which get removed
  • Widgets per user
  • Drag between rows and columns, the friction point the study found
  • Old and new toggle, in both directions
  • Deep links out of individual widgets
  • Anything that distinguishes the new homepage from the old one
The only item with a deadline

PX feature tags bind to selectors. If the new markup ships without stable hooks, tagging becomes a scavenger hunt against generated class names. Stable data-* attributes on the nine elements above cost close to nothing during the build and cannot be added cheaply afterward. The data-slot pattern already in the prototype is the precedent.

Separately, the homepage to reporting and self loop baselines have to be captured against the current page. That is a pull rather than a build, so it can happen any time before launch, but it expires at launch.

Caveats on the numbers

  • The role sample hit the 20,000 record ceiling, so shares are of that sample rather than of the full tenant. It also spans GLM and SLM, and the blank bucket may include users from either.
  • Applicants are portal side and do not appear in Admin Homepage feature matches, so they inflate the role denominator relative to the homepage audience. Read role shares as a population picture, not as a homepage audience picture.
  • Grid share percentages use that week's homepage users as the denominator. A user who visits the homepage and a grid in different weeks is not double counted within a week but is across the eight week totals.
  • Grouping a module by its child features returned a server error, so the four grids were queried individually. Not a blocker, just slower.
  • Two accounts sampled for the role field check. Field presence is confirmed. Coverage rates across the base are not.
